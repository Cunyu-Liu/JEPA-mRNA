Corpus is a zip of zips; TE protocol is impractical as specified

Two findings from actually running the pipeline against the real artefacts.

1. The released pre-training archive is a zip containing five zips, each holding one
   FASTA (0.fasta alone is 21.8 GB). The reader treated those members as FASTA text and
   parsed compressed bytes as sequence -- the verify stage then reported mismatches
   against binary garbage. iter_fasta_from_zip now recurses into nested archives,
   spilling each inner zip to the work directory one at a time (a nested zip needs random
   access and the members are deflated, so a stream will not do) and deleting it after
   consumption, which bounds peak extra disk at the largest member instead of the whole
   21 GB. Verified on the real corpus: 5000 sequences, ORF mismatches 0, token mismatches
   0 against the official implementation.

2. ultra-long TE cannot be run as published. At the 3066-token budget with batch 1 and
   20 epochs it is 178,440 optimizer steps, and the quadratic attention needs 450 MiB of
   scores per layer per sample, which OOMs on a shared card. Registry updated to batch 2
   x 3 epochs (13,383 steps) with a protocol_note recording the deviation: the shortened
   protocol is applied identically to every model compared, so the delta stays valid
   while the absolute value is not comparable to the paper's 20-epoch number.

Also: the corpus download finished and its md5 matches the published value exactly
(bf8bc5c946a0bd3b07716b1c7f785d54, 20,021,485,711 bytes). The last 15 chunks had to be
recovered with one connection per missing chunk: the 168-way fan-out had hit what
behaves like per-client throttling -- single-chunk fetches to three different Zenodo IPs
timed out while it was running, and dropping to ~14 connections recovered 12 of 14
chunks in six minutes.
