49x faster ORF search, and extract the corpus FASTAs first

Two throughput fixes for pre-processing, both measured.

1. The ORF search was the real bottleneck, not I/O. The official rule is simply "for every
   ATG the ORF ends at the first in-frame stop at or after start+3; take the longest, ties
   to the smallest start", which can be evaluated over codon *position lists* obtained
   with str.find (C speed) instead of walking characters in Python. On 200 real sequences
   averaging 4,077 nt: 57 seq/s -> 2,773 seq/s, a 48.7x speedup. Equivalence was
   re-verified after the rewrite, not assumed: 17 adversarial cases, 4,000 skewed-alphabet
   random sequences and all 22,671 sequences of the official sample FASTA still match the
   official implementation exactly, and the pipeline's own verify stage reports
   ORF mismatches 0 / token mismatches 0 on 20,000 sequences sampled from the real corpus.

2. scripts/extract_corpus.sh extracts the five FASTAs once (~99 GB) so pre-processing reads
   plain files. Reading them straight out of the nested zip made the reader single-threaded
   and CPU-bound at roughly 10-19 MB/s under contention; extraction runs at 150-160 MB/s
   because it stays inside zlib's C path. The pipeline prefers pre-extracted FASTAs when
   present and falls back to the archive otherwise.

Also worth recording, because it changes the picture: the real corpus averages 2,021 tokens
per sequence against 413 for the official sample, i.e. ~89 billion tokens in total. That is
why plain head truncation was replaced with a head-or-tail crop (previous commit): at
max_len 512 the 3'UTR would essentially never have been seen.

Observed after both fixes: 8,000 sequences/s end to end, pre.txt growing at ~2.6 GB/min,
and a spot check of the first 20,000 output lines shows 0 token/region misalignments with
a balanced region distribution (CDS 3.66M, 5'UTR 1.57M, 3'UTR 6.11M tokens).
